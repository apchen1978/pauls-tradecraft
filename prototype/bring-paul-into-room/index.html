Bring Paul Into the Room

A short exercise for the moment when an organization has a solution in mind — and the decision underneath it has not been examined yet.

Illustrative scenario · not a client result

01 / THE REQUEST

Start with the answer you already have.

Founder says
“We want an AI sales agent.”

Paul does not answer yes or no. He asks for one recent decision that still feels expensive, unclear, or difficult to explain.

Why start here? A solution request can hide several different problems: speed, attention, judgment, capacity, or commitment.

02 / WHAT HAPPENED

Follow one opportunity, before judging the system.

SYNTHETIC INDUSTRIAL EXPORTERA promising overseas RFQ moves through the company before the important conditions are clear.

Day 1RFQ arrivesThe opportunity looks promising. Several specifications are still open.
Day 2Engineering is pulled inCustomization is explored before the scope is stable.
Day 3Founder joins pricingCommercial judgment is requested early.
Day 4Production checks feasibilityDelivery questions now carry operational cost.
Day 6A missing condition appearsThe buyer reveals an unresolved requirement. The opportunity stalls while both sides clarify.

The question is changing. The team did not simply fail to follow up. It committed several kinds of attention while the opportunity was still moving.

03 / INSIDE THE DECISION

What was known, assumed, and still unknown?

What was known

Within this scenario, a buyer had made a request, creating a possible commercial opportunity.

What was assumed

The customization was worth exploring before the commercial conditions were stable.

What was unknown

The buyer’s final requirement, delivery fit, and the true cost of responding were not yet clear.

What was committed

Engineering attention, Founder time, pricing judgment, and a production feasibility check.

No score is needed. The useful question is where meaningful organizational commitment actually began — and whether it began too early.

04 / THE REFRAME

We may not have an AI problem.

We may have a decision problem that AI could help us see — or handle differently.

Don’t build the agent yet.

Not because AI is useless. Because faster automation could simply accelerate the wrong commitment.

05 / BEFORE BUILDING

Make the next move reversible.

Review a small set of recent opportunities and locate where meaningful organizational commitment actually began.

  • What did the team know at the time?
  • What was assumed too early?
  • Which resource or promise became difficult to reverse?
  • What would prove this reframe wrong?

Only then decide whether an AI intervention is justified — and what narrow job it should do.

06 / LEAVE WITH A BETTER QUESTION
“Bring one difficult decision — not an AI brief. Let’s find out what actually needs to change.”

Paul’s first contribution may be a clearer decision, not a faster system.

What this demonstrates

Judgment
