我的工作方法

海外業務開發最貴的錯，通常不是動作太慢，而是在證據還不夠的時候，就先對客戶做了承諾。這頁說明我在找買家、評估機會、報價與交接時，固定遵守的做法。

海外開發 · 報價 · 交接
聊聊你的海外開發 回到工作方式

證據

每一項判斷都要指得出來源；指不出來的，就標成未知。

核准

價格、交期與付款條件，說出口之前先確認由誰同意。

檢查

報價、信件與附件，寄出前再核對一次數字與條件。

紀錄

留下別人接得起來的紀錄：為什麼這樣判斷、還缺什麼。

五個固定做法

  1. 已知、推論、未知分開寫 — 買方公開寫出來的、我推測的、還沒確認的，各放一欄。未知就寫未知，不寫成「應該可以」。
  2. 承諾之前，先講好核准點 — 價格、交期、付款條件、樣品費，哪一項由誰拍板，在接觸客戶前就講清楚。判斷不了的，先停下來問。
  3. 看內容的效果，不看動作大小 — 同樣是一封信，可能只是問候，也可能等於報價。會形成承諾的內容，一律先經過核准。
  4. 寄出前再查一次 — 單價、幣別、數量、交貨條件與附件逐項對照；和先前說過的不一致，先修正再寄。
  5. 紀錄要能交接 — 每個案子都留下目前的判斷、依據、還沒解決的問題、下一步與負責人。換人接手時，不必從頭再問一次。

一個常見情境：詢價資訊不完整

一份海外詢價只寫了品名和大概數量，沒有規格、沒有目標價，也看不出對方在採購裡扮演什麼角色。這時我不急著報價，而是先把已知與未知列出來：哪些可以從公開資料確認，哪些只能問買方。

接著擬一份簡短的澄清問題，同時確認公司內部能接受的價格區間與交期，經過核准才回覆。如果買方後來補充的資訊改變了條件，例如數量變多或交期提前，原本的判斷就重新檢視，不沿用舊的核准。

找買家候選名單，以及每一家的判斷依據對方實際的採購角色與時機

這套做法的範圍

這是我的工作習慣，不是保證成交的流程。每個市場、產品與買家都不同，做法要依實際情況調整。

稅務、法律與合約條款的結論，仍由相關專業人士確認。

對公司的意義

很多團隊在海外開發卡住，不是不夠努力，而是判斷散在各人腦中，承諾在不知不覺中就說出口了。把證據、核准點與紀錄放進同一個流程，團隊才能做得快，也接得起來。

HOW I WORK

How I work

The most expensive mistake in overseas business development is rarely moving too slowly. It is making a commitment to a customer before the evidence is there. This page sets out the practices I follow when finding buyers, assessing opportunities, quoting, and handing work over.

Business development · Quoting · Handover

Evidence

Every judgment should point to a source. If it cannot, it is marked as unknown.

Approval

Before price, lead time, or payment terms are mentioned to a buyer, it is clear who signs them off.

Check

Quotes, emails, and attachments get one more check of figures and terms before they go out.

Records

Records someone else can pick up: why a call was made and what is still missing.

Five working practices

  1. Keep facts, inferences, and unknowns apart — what the buyer has stated publicly, what I infer, and what is still unconfirmed each get their own column. Unknown stays unknown; it is never written up as "should be fine".
  2. Agree approval points before any commitment — price, lead time, payment terms, sample charges: who decides each one is settled before we contact the buyer. When something cannot be judged, I stop and ask.
  3. Judge a message by its effect, not its size — the same email can be a courtesy note or, in effect, a quotation. Anything that creates a commitment goes through approval first.
  4. Check before sending — unit price, currency, quantity, delivery terms, and attachments are checked line by line. If anything contradicts what was said before, it is fixed before sending.
  5. Keep records that can be handed over — every case keeps the current judgment, the evidence behind it, open questions, the next step, and who owns it. Whoever takes over does not have to start from scratch.

A common situation: an incomplete enquiry

An overseas enquiry arrives with a product name and a rough quantity, but no specification, no target price, and no sign of the sender's role in purchasing. Instead of rushing a quote, I first list what is known and what is not: what can be confirmed from public sources, and what only the buyer can answer.

I then draft a short set of clarifying questions, confirm the price range and lead time we can accept internally, and reply only once that is approved. If the buyer later adds information that changes the picture, such as a larger quantity or an earlier delivery date, the earlier judgment is reviewed again rather than carried over under the old approval.

What each stage leaves behind

Scope

These are my working habits, not a process that guarantees a sale. Every market, product, and buyer is different, and the approach is adjusted to the situation.

Tax, legal, and contract conclusions remain with the relevant professionals.

Why it matters to a company

Many teams stall in overseas development not for lack of effort, but because judgment lives in individual heads and commitments get made without anyone noticing. Putting evidence, approval points, and records into one process lets a team move quickly and still hand work over cleanly.
