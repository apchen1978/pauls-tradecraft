## Mission

Produce one concise recommendation that:

1. Inventories the current works by name and their apparent commercial idea.
2. Distinguishes works that feel **deep / original / memorable** from those that feel **shallow / conventional / duplicative**. Use direct editorial judgment, not politeness.
3. Recommends **DEEPEN / MERGE / RETIRE** candidates. Do not preserve weak works to protect portfolio count.
4. Finds the **hidden creative embryos** already present: unusual insights, combinations, artifacts, or interaction ideas that could become much more than their current framing.
5. Proposes **3–5 non-obvious but commercially plausible Garage prototype directions**. Look for possibilities such as weak signals becoming R&D hypotheses, emerging-market clues becoming product opportunities, factory capabilities meeting unexpected buyers/products, AI-assisted opportunity sensing, or fast beautiful market experiments—but do not merely repeat these examples.
6. Prioritizes **one cheapest interesting prototype to build first**. Prefer a small, inspectable prototype that reuses existing knowledge or assets, creates a genuine discovery moment, and can be tested without backend, auth, production integration, or a website redesign.
7. Explains the prototype in concrete terms: the surprising connection, who would care, what the visitor would discover or do, the smallest artifact to build, and what reality check would come next.

For every meaningful existing work, ask:

- **What is the hidden treasure here?**
- **What makes someone see the problem differently?**

Favor the experience arc:

**curiosity → discovery → unexpected connection → possibility → prototype / commercial move**

Avoid defaulting to:

**problem → framework → analysis → homework → next step**

## Grounded Imagination boundary

Ideas may exist before pilot-level evidence. Label speculative, synthetic, incomplete, and unvalidated material honestly; do not smother promising ideas with premature proof demands.

Hard facts remain hard facts. Never invent or blur:

- customer facts, adoption, demand, outcomes, ROI, revenue, or conversion
- market evidence or research findings
- Incoterms, accounting, taxation, law, specifications, pricing, or financial numbers
- prototype behavior, validation, or production readiness

Separate **observed repository evidence**, **creative inference**, and **unknowns**. A provocative possibility is welcome; a fabricated reality is not.
